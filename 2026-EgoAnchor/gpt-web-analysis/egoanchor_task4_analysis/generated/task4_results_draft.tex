\paragraph{Task 4: continuous rotation.}
After a 14.4-s setup interval, the sequence contained three
marker-defined continuous-rotation segments. We treated each segment as one
paired analysis unit and evaluated frames with platform-reference angular speed
of at least 20$^\circ$/s. Relative to One-Euro
Anchor, EgoAnchor reduced the median segment rotation error from
14.2$^\circ$ to
13.2$^\circ$, with the same
direction in all three segments. The median segment P95 decreased from
22.8$^\circ$ to
22.3$^\circ$, although only
two of the three segments improved, so we treat this tail result as descriptive.

\paragraph{Rotation lag and continuity.}
Trajectory-shift analysis estimated median effective rotation lags of
265,
255,
380,
and 340 ms
for Arrival-Hold, Capture-Hold, One-Euro Anchor, and EgoAnchor, respectively.
EgoAnchor reduced effective lag relative to One-Euro Anchor in all three
segments and also reduced the lag-compensated residual from
2.61$^\circ$
to 2.48$^\circ$.
Its median P99 angular jump was
2.34$^\circ$,
compared with
6.60$^\circ$
for One-Euro Anchor.

\paragraph{Design attribution.}
Temporal synthesis reduced the median segment P99 angular jump from
8.02$^\circ$ to
2.34$^\circ$
(71\%), but added
75 ms effective rotation lag and
increased raw median rotation error by
2.6$^\circ$. Capture-time alignment reduced raw
median rotation error from 17.7$^\circ$ to
13.2$^\circ$ and reduced effective lag by
145
ms. VCD accepted all candidates in this continuously visible trial, and the
corresponding ablation was numerically indistinguishable, serving as an expected
negative control.
